---
title: Use a 3D-Printed HDD Tray
---

Add a second hard drive to the Fractal Design Define R5 case in `scarif`, the NAS described in the [hardware reference](../reference/hardware.md), using a 3D-printed tray when you don't have a second official metal tray. This guide records the modifications needed for the [Fractal Define R5 HDD tray](https://www.printables.com/model/79789-fractal-define-r5-hdd-tray) model on Printables.

## Before you begin

- A 3D-printed copy of the tray.
- A pair of pliers and a Bosch No. 4 HSS PointTeQ drill bit. No drill is needed.
- A tool to cut the rubber grommets.
- The hard drive's mounting screws and rubber anti-vibration grommets.

## Enlarge the screw holes

The screw holes in the printed tray were slightly too small, so the screws wouldn't fit.

1. Hold the drill bit with the pliers.
1. Screw the bit into each screw hole by hand.
1. Check that the screws fit and repeat for the remaining holes.

{{< callout type="info" >}}
  This worked well.
{{< /callout >}}

## Trim the rubber grommets

The grommets from the official trays sit on both sides of the tray. The printed tray is much thicker than the official metal one, so they don't seat fully in the holes.

1. Cut off the outer half of each grommet.
1. Fit the remaining half on the inside of the tray, between the drive and the tray.

## Mount the drive

1. Place the drive on the tray.
1. Screw the drive in through the trimmed grommets.
1. Slide the tray into a free drive bay.

The tray fits snugly and slides in and out. It's harder to pull out than the official metal trays, but still usable.
